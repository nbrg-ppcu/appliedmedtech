# The marker game

---

## Where we are

The lecture asked one question of every marker: **why is it linked to the disease, and will that
reason still hold where you use it?** Today you ask it of proteins nobody has explained to you.

## The study

A hospital ran a coronary artery disease study: 600 patients and 600 people without the disease, from
2 cohorts, blood taken after diagnosis. The lab measured the markers you know from the lecture, and
**200 further proteins** from a large protein panel. The hospital wants to know which of those 200
matter, for a diagnostic panel, for a follow-up list, or as a drug target.

**Why the proteins have no names.** In a real study you would know every name, and the first thing
anyone does is look it up. We hid the names on purpose: today the only way to learn what a protein does
is to ask the data, which is the skill this session is about. At the end you get the names, the
stories, and a score.

## How it runs

- **Decide, run, explain.** Each task is a decision; the toolkit does the mechanics; you write one
  sentence at the end of each part, in the notebook.
- **Write down what you believe before you test it.** The notebook asks for it at two points. A guess
  you wrote down teaches more than one you only remember.
- **Your verdicts.** The last task collects, for every protein you believe matters, what kind of link it
  has to the disease and what it is good for. Then the roles are revealed and your verdicts are scored:
  1 point per correct kind of link, one per correct use. The scoring rules are the lecture.

## What to hand in
